EY Business Transformation · March 2026

Every decade, the platform shifts.
This is the agentic one.

The world runs on technology platforms. Every major shift — mainframe to ERP, ERP to cloud — redrew which companies won and which got left behind. We are in the next one now. Agentic AI does not augment the operating model. It replaces workflow layers entirely.

1960s–80s
Mainframe
Centralized compute. IT as infrastructure.
1980s–2000s
ERP Era
SAP, Oracle. Workflow digitization at scale.
2000s–2015
Cloud / SaaS
Global delivery. API economy. Mobile first.
2015–2022
AI / ML
Predictive models. Automation layer one.
Now → 2027
Agentic AI
Autonomous workflows. Function layers replaced. The operating model is the variable.

"We're starting to see projects that used to require big teams now be accomplished by a single very talented person."

Mark Zuckerberg · Q4 2025 Earnings · $META

Three signals EY cannot ignore

Signal · Competitive threat

OpenAI partnered with BCG, McKinsey, Accenture, and Capgemini to deploy enterprise AI at scale — each building a dedicated practice with direct product and research support. EY was not included.

These are not legacy firms adapting. They are starting clean — no partnership model, no hourly economics, no prior constraints on delivery speed.
Signal · Compression
Implementation cycles are collapsing
Palantir and SAP will do implementations in weeks, not quarters. The hours-based model is not under pressure — it is being structurally replaced.

What survives is what requires genuine human judgment: misaligned incentives, political complexity, behavioral change. That is EY's right to win — if we move.

The human gap

The technical gap is closing.
The human gap is not.

AI can generate a process map, a benchmarking report, and a value case in minutes. What it cannot do is navigate a CFO who does not trust the assumptions, a CHRO whose team is threatened, or a Board that has seen three transformations fail.

EY right to win

Politics, misaligned incentives, unclear ownership, and institutional inertia have not received the software upgrade. The gap between what is technically possible and what organizations can actually absorb may now be greater than ever. That is EY's moat — if we build the tools to get in the room fast and the credibility to hold it.

What survives
Complex, multi-stakeholder programs where human judgment, trust, and behavioral change determine outcomes — not just the technology selection.
What gets compressed
Advisory work that is primarily analysis, benchmarking, or recommendation — anything an agent can generate with the right data access.
The model that wins
Outcome-based. Tool-enabled. Bespoke. Deployed in days. Paid on value realized — not hours sold.

Phase 02 · HOW · EY Delivery Teams

The strategy is approved.
Now we need a plan that holds.

Once the big rocks are identified, the org needs a credible execution plan. Technology, process, and people change must be sequenced correctly — or value leaks at every handoff. This is where most transformations fail.

What Phase 02 delivers
Program architecture
Workstream definition, sequencing, dependency mapping. What goes first, what depends on what, who owns each stream.
Technology scoping
For SAP S/4HANA programs: Phase 0 visioning, configuration approach, hybrid architecture decisions, custom object strategy. Built with the client, not for the client.
Behavioral change layer
The critical behaviors required for each initiative to land. Not change management as a checkbox — prescriptive, evidence-based behavioral requirements per workstream.
Value realization setup
KPIs, baselines, and measurement cadence established in Phase 02 — so Phase 04 has something to track against. Value realization starts here, not at go-live.
The stakeholder reality at this stage

"The strategy is approved. Now I need a credible plan — what goes first, what depends on what, and who owns it. I have been through transformations where this was not clear and we lost 18 months."

— COO, 90 days into a transformation mandate
EY sector advantage
EY delivery teams bring deep sector-specific implementation knowledge — not generic methodology. A healthcare payer S/4HANA program is structurally different from an aerospace manufacturer. The sequencing, the compliance requirements, the integration points — all sector-specific.

This is where EY's industry depth creates differentiated value versus pure-play technology integrators.
What changes in the agentic era
AI agents can now handle the analytical and documentation layer of program architecture in days, not weeks. The human role shifts to judgment, sequencing decisions, stakeholder alignment, and behavioral change. EY teams that are built around this shift will outpace those still selling the analytical hours.
